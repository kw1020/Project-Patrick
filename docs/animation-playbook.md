# Animation Playbook — Video Games Explain the Real World

How to make the videos look better, faster, and cheaper. Start at Level 1 and
move up as the channel grows.

## The big wins (do these first, any game)

1. **Hook in the first 1–3 seconds.** Open on the most dramatic shot, not a
   title card. Retention matters more than animation quality.
2. **Consistent host character.** Same look, same voice, every video. This is
   what turns viewers into subscribers (and later sells merch).
3. **Camera movement.** Slow pushes, orbits, and whip-pans make even simple
   scenes feel cinematic. Static shots feel cheap.
4. **Animate the "real world" part with overlays.** Charts, arrows, numbers,
   and labels on top of the game footage are what make it an *explainer*.
5. **Sound design.** Game sound effects + whooshes + background music make
   animation feel 2x better for almost no work.
6. **Build reusable templates** (intro, lower thirds, chart styles, transitions)
   so every episode is faster than the last.

## Levels

### Level 1 — Record real gameplay + edit (free, fastest)
- Play the game, capture with **OBS Studio** (free).
- Edit in **CapCut** (fastest for Shorts) or **DaVinci Resolve** (free, pro).
- Add text, charts, zooms, and sound effects on top.

### Level 2 — Cinematic in-game capture
- Stage scenes in the game, then fly the camera through them afterwards with
  a replay tool (see per-game list below).
- Use shaders / texture packs for a better look.

### Level 3 — Real 3D / 2D animation
- **Blender** (free) for full 3D control of characters and cameras.
- **Mixamo** (free) for ready-made character animations.
- **AI motion capture from phone video:** Rokoko Video, Move.ai, DeepMotion —
  act the scene out yourself, turn it into character motion.

### Level 4 — AI-assisted production (Patrick's agents)
- **Voice:** ElevenLabs (or similar) for a consistent host voice.
- **Lip sync / talking head:** Adobe Character Animator, or AI lip-sync tools.
- **AI video generation** (Runway, Kling, Google Veo, and whatever leads the
  market when we start) for B-roll, transitions, and real-world cutaways —
  not for copying game characters.
- **AI music** only from tools whose license allows commercial use.
- Patrick's agents write the script, generate the shot list, voice it, and
  assemble a rough cut; Krew reviews and approves.

## Per-game tools

| Game | Best capture/animation tools |
|---|---|
| **Minecraft** | **Mine-imator** (free, easiest Minecraft animator); **Blender + MCprep** addon (pro-level); **Replay Mod / Flashback** (record then re-shoot camera paths); **Mineways / jmc2obj** (export your world into Blender); shaders like Complementary or BSL |
| **Terraria** | 2D style: sprite animation in **Aseprite**; puppet animation in **Moho**, **Spine**, or **Adobe Animate**; **tModLoader** for custom in-game scenes |
| **Roblox** | **Roblox Studio** built-in animation editor + Moon Animator plugin; export to Blender for cinematics |
| **Fortnite** | **UEFN (Unreal Editor for Fortnite)** to build custom scenes; **Replay mode** for cinematic cameras; **Unreal Engine 5 Sequencer** for full cinematics |
| **Call of Duty** | **Theater / replay mode** where available; gameplay capture + heavy editing, zooms, and overlays |
| **Pokémon-style** | Gameplay capture + commentary, **or** original creatures drawn/animated in 2D (Moho, Toon Boom Harmony, Adobe Animate) to avoid Nintendo issues |
| **Mario-style** | Gameplay capture + commentary, **or** original platformer-style characters in 2D/3D |

## Learning path for Krew

1. Week 1–2: OBS + CapCut. Make 5 Minecraft shorts (vertical). Focus on hooks.
2. Week 3–4: Mine-imator. Animate a simple 30-second scene with the host.
3. Month 2: Replay Mod/Flashback + shaders for cinematic shots.
4. Month 3: Blender basics (Blender Guru's donut tutorial) + MCprep.
5. After that: bring in AI mocap and AI voice so Patrick's agents can produce
   more of the pipeline.

## Quality checklist (before every upload)

- [ ] Hook in first 1–3 seconds
- [ ] Real-world fact checked
- [ ] Camera is moving in most shots
- [ ] Text/charts explain the real-world concept clearly
- [ ] Sound effects + music (commercial-use licensed)
- [ ] Follows that game's fan-content policy
- [ ] Vertical 9:16, ~30–60s, burned-in captions
- [ ] Exported for YouTube Shorts, Instagram Reels, and TikTok
