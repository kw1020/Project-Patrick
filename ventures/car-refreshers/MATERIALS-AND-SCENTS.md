# Materials and Scents

What each line is made of, which scents to launch, and what to demand from suppliers. Decisions are marked **Pick**. Anything not backed by a source is labeled as judgment or as something to test.

## Three facts that drive every choice

1. **Cars get hot.** An ASU / UC San Diego study found dashboards in sun-parked cars average about 157°F after an hour and pass 165°F. Cabin air averaged about 116°F. Every material has to survive 165°F+ without melting, leaking or smelling burnt.
2. **Fragrance oil damages car plastic.** Febreze's own vent clip warns to avoid contact with the dashboard, and car-care guides say even a little concentrated oil can permanently mark it. So nothing liquid, and every refill must be dry to the touch.
3. **Rich people's cars often have odd vents.** Tesla Model 3/Y have a slit vent that standard clips don't fit; Spigen sells an adapter just for this. Round, deep-set or styled vents (Mercedes, some exotics) also don't hold standard clips securely. The hardware needs more than one way to mount.

## Refill carrier (the part that holds the scent)

| Material | Life (seller claims) | Heat | Feel | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Gel | 3–4 weeks | Can melt around 140°F | Cheap | **No.** Fails fact 1. |
| EVA polymer beads/disc | Long, up to 30% oil by weight | Melts in heat (seller warning) | Plastic | **No** for the vent. Too close to dash temperatures. |
| Paper pulp board | 2–4 weeks | Fine | Cheap but proven (classic hanging tree) | **Pick for Daily** |
| Wood (alder/basswood) | 4–8 weeks | Fine | Natural, warm | Backup for Daily, but Drift already owns the "wood" look |
| Polyester felt | 2–3 weeks | ~158°F max | Fine | Cheap backup for Signature |
| Wool felt | Similar, more porous | Better than polyester | Soft | Backup for Signature |
| Porous ceramic / stone | 3–5 weeks | Won't melt | Heavy, cool, premium | **Pick for Signature and Reserve** |

**Picks:**
- **Daily:** Thick scented paper-pulp card in a sealed foil sleeve. The buyer pulls it out a little at a time to control strength, which is what makes these last.
- **Signature:** **Porous ceramic disc**, matte black, logo pressed in. It's dry, can't melt or drip, and feels expensive in the hand. Customers can also top it up with a few drops of oil, which suits a "Refill Oil" add-on later.
- **Reserve:** **Black porous stone insert** (sintered ceramic or natural lava stone) loaded with a higher dose of fine-fragrance oil. Same no-drip benefits, more weight and story.

None of the longevity numbers above come from lab tests; they're seller claims. Run the 30-day test below before ordering.

## Clip body (the hardware)

| | Signature | Reserve |
|---|---|---|
| Metal | **Zinc alloy (zamak), die-cast.** Heavy and cheap at volume. | **Solid brass first, titanium later.** |
| Why | The supplier's existing mold plus your logo engraved avoids a custom mold fee at the start | Brass machines far more easily than titanium and the raw metal costs about a quarter as much, so a 50-piece run is realistic. It's heavy and darkens nicely with age. Titanium is lighter and more prestigious but much slower and pricier to machine, so it stays a later "Titanium Edition." |
| Finish | **PVD** for gold and black (much harder and longer-wearing than standard plating; zinc needs a base plate first). Ask suppliers if they offer it. | Raw/brushed brass (ages over time), or PVD black. Color-matched enamel face as an option. |
| Est. cost | $3–6 at low MOQ | $18–45 machined at 25–100 pcs (get 3 quotes from the same drawing) |

**Mounting system (Signature and Reserve), my design call:**
- The face holds the scent disc and snaps onto a **magnetic base**.
- The box includes two bases: a **hook-style vent clip** (grips behind the louver rather than pinching it, so it works on more vents and doesn't sag) and a **low-profile adhesive mount** for Tesla slit vents, round vents, or cars where the owner won't clip anything to the vent.
- Weight warning: a heavy metal face can sag or snap a vent louver. Keep the vent-mounted part as light as practical and put the weight in the face. Test on real cars before ordering.

**Vent fit to check in person** (detail shops are where to do this): Mercedes round vents (C/E/S/G-Class), Porsche 911/Cayenne, Range Rover, Rolls-Royce, Lamborghini Urus, Tesla Model 3/Y (old and new). I couldn't find reliable fit data for most of these, so measure them.

## Packaging

- **Daily:** Printed card + foil sleeve. Matte black, small "SMP Daily" logo, no gold.
- **Signature:** Rigid black box, gold-foil logo, molded insert holding clip + 2 discs.
- **Reserve:** Leather case inside a rigid magnetic box, hand-signed card, numbered certificate.

## Scents

### What the research says
- **Leather, oud and tobacco are the luxury lane right now.** Reviews of 2026 releases say tobacco and leather accords are having a moment in luxury circles, and oud keeps showing up as a top note to watch.
- **Mercedes proves the rich pay for car scent.** Mercedes sells factory car fragrances for its Air-Balance system. Its oud scent, **Agarwood Mood**, developed for Maybach, is listed at **A$163.90 for a 15 ml bottle**. That supports the Reserve price and tells you oud belongs in the luxury car.
- **Tobacco-vanilla car scents already sell** to buyers who want rich, sensual scents (e.g. Vilicci), so there's demand, but you need your own version.

### Strength per line (judgment)
- **Daily: strong.** Cheap-freshener buyers want to smell it immediately.
- **Signature: medium.** Noticeable when you get in, not overwhelming.
- **Reserve: soft.** In luxury, a loud scent signals cheap. Aim for "the car smells expensive," not "the car smells like a freshener."

### Where the oils come from
| Line | Source | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Daily | Stock fragrance oils from US wholesale suppliers | Bulk synthetic oils run around $16.50/lb at 15 lb, so cents per card |
| Signature | Premium stock oils, lightly customized | Custom blends usually carry higher minimums (500–1,000 units) |
| Reserve | Commissioned from an independent perfumer or small fragrance lab | Start with premium stock oils for the prototypes, commission once Reserve sells |

Leather scents use materials such as isobutyl quinoline and birch tar. Birch tar is restricted under IFRA, and sources disagree on the exact limits, so leave this to the perfumer and get the IFRA certificate.

## What every fragrance supplier must give you

- [ ] **IFRA certificate covering Category 12.** Solid, non-aerosol car diffusers and cartridge fresheners are Category 12, which has no use limit (sprays fall under stricter 10A/10B).
- [ ] **SDS (safety data sheet).**
- [ ] **Flash point above 200°F**, above the 157–165°F dashboard temperatures. Avoid oils heavy in citrus essential oils; sweet orange is around 129°F (54°C).
- [ ] **Phthalate-free.**
- [ ] **Allergen list.** California's Cleaning Product Right to Know Act covers air care products. Fragrance allergens above 0.01% must be disclosed, either listed by name or with "Contains fragrance allergen(s)" on the label plus a full ingredient list online. Check current dates against the statute; summaries differ.
- [ ] **Prop 65 statement** (whether any listed chemical is present).

## 30-day test before ordering

Cheap and quick, and it replaces the seller claims with your own numbers.
1. Buy samples of: paper pulp card, wool felt disc, polyester felt disc, porous ceramic disc, lava stone.
2. Load each with the **same oil** (Midnight Leather sample) and the same amount.
3. Weigh each on a $15 jewelry scale (0.01 g) on day 0, 3, 7, 14, 21, 30. Weight lost = oil gone.
4. Rate the smell 1–10 at the same times, getting into the car after it's been parked.
5. Leave one of each on the dashboard in the sun for a day: check for melting, sweating or residue.
6. Test clip grip and sag on 5 different cars (yours + detail shop customers).

The carrier that is still above 5/10 on day 30, with no residue, wins.
