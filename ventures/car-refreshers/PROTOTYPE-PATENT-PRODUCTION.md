# Prototype, Patent, Production

How Sutherland Motor Parfum goes from the Design Lab to boxes on a shelf. Fees were checked in November 2026; costs marked "estimate" are rough and need real quotes. This is not legal advice.

## 1. Getting a prototype

Build three kinds of prototype, cheapest first.

| Prototype | What it proves | How | Rough cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Supplier samples** (Daily + Signature) | The real product works: grip, weight, scent, heat | Order samples with the supplier messages. Their existing molds plus your logo already are your prototype. | $20–80 per supplier plus shipping (estimate) |
| **Looks-like model** (any custom shape) | Size, shape and feel in the hand and on a vent | 3D model → resin 3D print from an online print service or a local makerspace → paint gold or black | $10–50 per print (estimate) |
| **Reserve brass prototype** | The luxury piece is as good as the price | 3D model (STEP file) → CNC shop quote for 3 pieces → engrave and finish | Get 3 quotes (Xometry, PCBWay, JLCCNC, local shop) |

**The 3D model (CAD file)** is the key step for anything custom. Two ways:
- Learn Fusion 360 (free for personal use). A round clip face is a beginner project.
- Hire a product designer on Upwork or Fiverr to model it from the Design Lab spec. A simple part is usually a few hundred dollars (estimate).

Then run the **30-day test** from MATERIALS-AND-SCENTS.md on the samples before ordering anything in bulk.

## 2. Patent and protection

**The honest answer: a patent is probably not where your protection comes from.**

- **Utility patent** (protects how something works): vent-clip fresheners are an old, crowded field. There are many existing patents for clip-on and adjustable vent fresheners, so a basic clip won't qualify. You'd need a mechanism that is truly new. With an attorney these usually cost many thousands of dollars and take years. **Skip it unless you invent a new mechanism.**
- **Design patent** (protects how something looks): this is realistic for a distinctive design, such as the Reserve shield face. Plenty of vent-clip design patents exist already, so yours must look clearly different.
  - USPTO fees: about **$520 to file (filing + search + examination) + $520 when granted** as a small entity, or **$260 + $260** if you qualify as a micro entity. The USPTO has warned about people wrongly claiming micro status, so check that you qualify.
  - Add professional patent drawings, and an attorney or agent if you use one.
  - It lasts 15 years from grant, with no maintenance fees.
  - When: once the Reserve design is final and **before** you show it publicly. The US gives a 1-year grace period after you go public, but most other countries give none.
- **Trademark** (protects the name): **this is the one that matters most.** People copy products; your brand name is what they can't copy.
  - USPTO base application: **$350 per class**. Car fragrances likely fall in Class 3 (air fragrancing preparations) and/or Class 5 (air deodorizers); confirm in the USPTO ID Manual. Using the manual's wording avoids a $200-per-class surcharge.
  - Do a clearance search first (USPTO trademark search) for "Sutherland Motor Parfum" and close variants.
- **Practical protection**:
  - Move fast and build the brand.
  - Use Alibaba Trade Assurance.
  - Split Reserve work: a factory that also sells on Alibaba should not get your full luxury design if you can avoid it.
  - Keep the perfumer's formula owned by you (put it in the contract).

**Cheaper legal help:** the USPTO Patent Pro Bono Program and law school IP clinics help inventors and small businesses for little or nothing if you qualify.

**Recommendation:** file the trademark now (after a clearance search). Decide on a design patent for Reserve once the design is final and sales show it's worth protecting. Skip the utility patent.

## 3. Production and manufacturing

**Model: private label first, custom later.**
- **Daily + Signature:** use the supplier's existing molds with your logo, finish and packaging. No tooling cost. A custom die-cast mold comes later, once a design is selling. Molds often cost thousands (estimate).
- **Reserve:** made to order in small CNC batches, engraved per customer.

**Who puts the scent in?**
- **Daily:** the supplier pre-scents the cards with your approved oil and seals them in foil. This is cheapest at volume.
- **Signature + Reserve (start):** buy **unscented** discs and stones plus your own oil, and scent and pack them yourself for the first few hundred units. You control quality and can change scents without a new factory order. It also makes you the manufacturer for labeling purposes.

**The production steps**
1. **Samples** → 30-day test → pick the supplier.
2. **Golden sample:** the factory makes one final pre-production unit. You approve it in writing and keep it. Production must match it.
3. **Order + deposit:** typically 30% up front and 70% before shipping. Pay through Alibaba Trade Assurance or another protected method. Never wire 100% up front.
4. **Production:** usually 2–5 weeks for small runs.
5. **Inspection before shipping:** hire a third-party inspection company to check a sample of the order against the golden sample before you pay the balance. Usually a few hundred dollars per visit (estimate).
6. **Shipping:** air for small first orders (faster, pricier per kg), sea once volumes grow.
7. **Customs + tariffs:** look up the product's HTS code and the **current** tariff on goods from that country before you order. Tariffs on imports from China changed often in 2025–2026 and can change your cost a lot. A customs broker or freight forwarder handles the paperwork.
8. **Receive, scent, pack, label, ship to customers.**

**Business setup needed before the first production order**
- LLC + EIN, sales tax registration (resale certificate)
- Product liability insurance (needed before selling to shops)
- Labels: warnings, "Contains fragrance allergen(s)" for California, Prop 65 if any listed chemical is present, your business name and address
- UPC barcodes (GS1) if you'll sell on Amazon or in stores
- Supplier paperwork on file for every scent: IFRA certificate, SDS, flash point, allergen list
