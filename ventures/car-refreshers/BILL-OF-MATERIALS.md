# Bill of Materials and Invention Disclosure

Every part of the three Sutherland Motor Parfum air fresheners, what it's made of, and why. It also covers how the scent is released and made to last, and what might be patentable. Written so a supplier can quote from it and a patent attorney can start from it.

Status: design stage. Anything marked *target* or *estimate* must be confirmed with samples and the 30-day test (MATERIALS-AND-SCENTS.md). Not legal advice.

---

## Part A. How scent is released and made to last

A car freshener's strength and life come from five things. Each tier uses better versions of them.

| Lever | What it does | Daily | Signature | Reserve |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Carrier** (what holds the oil) | How much oil it holds and how evenly it lets go | Paper blotter board | Porous ceramic disc | **Sealed membrane cartridge** (liquid behind a microporous film) |
| **2. Release control** | Stops the first-week blast and the week-three fade | Foil pouch, pulled out gradually | Disc sits in a cradle; vent air passes over it | The membrane meters vapor at a near-steady rate, and the liquid can't escape |
| **3. Airflow path** | Vent air carries scent into the cabin | Hangs in open air | Rear lattice lets vent air through the disc | Rear lattice → across the membrane → out through a thin ring gap around the face (the "halo") |
| **4. Fixatives** (ingredients that slow evaporation) | Make the scent last longer and keep its shape | Standard musks and benzyl salicylate (supplier's choice) | Ambrox, Iso E Super-type woods, quality musks | Ambroxan, macrocyclic musks, labdanum and benzoin resins, natural oud/saffron/iris |
| **5. Diffusers** (ingredients that make it bloom) | Make the scent fill the cabin without being loud | Not specified | Hedione-type materials | Tuned by the perfumer |

**Why the cartridge wins for Reserve.** Liquid fragrance sits in a sealed reservoir. Vapor escapes through a microporous membrane while the liquid stays inside. Patents describe this as a near-constant ("zero-order") release rate. Vent air blowing across the membrane speeds release; when the car is parked it slows. That's exactly what a long-lasting luxury piece needs. The 60-day-type claims come from vendors, so ours must be measured. The basic membrane principle is decades old (US 4,948,047), so we use it; we don't patent it.

**Why not electronic (yet).** Waterless nebulizers atomize oil with compressed air and give the strongest scent with timers. But they need charging, and a lithium battery in a car whose dashboard reaches 157–165°F is a safety risk. Possible later "Atelier" accessory; not for launch.

**Heat rules for every tier**
- Finished oil flash point above 200°F; IFRA Category 12 certificate; SDS.
- **High-temperature magnets only.** Standard neodymium grades (N35–N52) are typically rated to about 176°F (80°C), right at dashboard heat. Use an **SH grade** (about 300°F/150°C rating), such as N42SH.
- No polystyrene or ABS touching oil, since fragrance oils attack some plastics. Use metal, glass, ceramic, PP, PET, POM (acetal) or nylon, and confirm compatibility with the oil supplier.

## Part C. Invention disclosure (for a patent attorney)

**Title (working):** Interchangeable-face vehicle fragrance diffuser with rear-lattice airflow, annular halo exit and magnetic membrane cartridge.

**Problem:**
- Vent fresheners either fade fast (paper, gel) or leak (liquid).
- They look cheap.
- They fit only standard vent slats.
- Swapping scents means throwing away the whole unit.

**The design:**
1. A decorative metal face (R1) that attaches by high-temperature magnets (R9) to one of several interchangeable bases: a vent hook with soft pads (R10/R11) or an adhesive base (R12).
2. Behind the face, a cradle with an open rear lattice (R6) holds a removable sealed membrane cartridge (R7).
3. Vent air enters through the lattice, passes across the membrane, and exits through a thin annular gap (R4) between face and rim. Scent leaves around the edge of the face rather than through visible holes.
4. Cartridges swap without tools; the face and bases are kept for life.
5. Optional: a rotating rim (R3) that varies the halo gap or lattice opening to set intensity.

**Drawings to prepare:** front, back, side, section through the center, and exploded view. Separate views of the cartridge and of each base.

**Best way to build it:** see the Part B materials. Include alternatives (stone insert instead of cartridge, titanium instead of brass, adhesive instead of hook), since an application should describe every version you might sell.

**What's probably new and what isn't (preliminary, from one round of searching):**
| Feature | Status |
|---|---|
| Membrane reservoir freshener | Known for decades (US 4,948,047). Free to use; not ours to claim. |
| Vent clip fresheners, many shapes | Crowded with design patents (e.g. D979037, D871564). |
| Rotating cap or body that sets intensity | Patented: US 11,433,155 (wick cap changes vent opening), US 7,140,553 (rotating cap), US 7,097,555 (front knob). **Check these before using R3.** |
| Interchangeable magnetic face + multi-base system + rear-lattice-to-halo airflow path + swappable membrane cartridge, combined | Not found in this search. **Possible** utility claim, depending on a professional search. |
| The Reserve's specific look (face, rim, halo) | **Design patent candidate** (about $520 + $520 USPTO fees at small-entity rates). |

**Next steps:**
1. Build the prototype and test the halo airflow (does scent output beat a plain disc?).
2. Patent attorney or agent runs a professional search in CPC class B60H 3/0028 (vehicle air fresheners) and checks whether we're free to use the rotating rim.
3. If there's something new, file a provisional before any public showing. It needs a description detailed enough for someone to build it, plus drawings; this document is the starting point.
4. File a design patent on the final Reserve look.
5. Keep the perfumer's formula owned by us (contract), as a trade secret.
